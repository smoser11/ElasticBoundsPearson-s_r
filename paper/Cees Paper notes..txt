  What the data are actually telling us                                         
                                                                                
  Finding 1: Asymmetry is a coin flip                                           
                                                                                
  Figure 7 is the most surprising result: the BES asymmetry distribution is     
  perfectly symmetric around zero — 50.0% of pairs each way. The same is true in
   the MC simulation (50.2%). This is not "asymmetry is caused by skewed        
  marginals." It's that the direction of asymmetry is essentially unpredictable
  from univariate marginal properties. The Corollary proves the condition for
  |r_min| > r_max, but empirically it cuts 50/50 in both random and real data.

  Implication for the paper: The asymmetry corollary should be framed as a      
  characterisation result ("here is what determines which side the asymmetry
  falls"), not as "skewed marginals produce |r_min| > r_max." The empirical     
  sections need to say this honestly.                       

  ---
  Finding 2: Entropy strongly predicts constraint severity but not asymmetry 
  direction                                                                     
           
  Figure 5a shows a clean, monotone, concave relationship: higher H̄ → higher C₁.
   This is a strong, publishable result that should go directly into §4/§5.     
  Figure 5b shows entropy tells you almost nothing about which direction the
  asymmetry goes — the cloud is symmetric around zero at every entropy level.   
  These are two distinct phenomena that need to be discussed separately.

  ---                                                                           
  Finding 3: More categories = substantially less constraint (Figure 4)
                                                                                
  The MC density plot by K is visually the clearest result in the whole
  analysis. At K=4, the C₁ distribution is spread from 0.2 to 1.0 with a peak   
  around 0.7. At K=11, almost everything is above 0.9. This is an important
  practical message: a 4-category Likert scale is systematically more           
  constrained than an 11-category scale, and pairs mixing different K values are
   inconsistently constrained.

  ---
  Finding 4: Rescaling has modest practical effect in BES (Figure 8a)
                                                                                
  The r_obs vs r* scatter sits very tightly around the diagonal. Rescaling
  adjusts most BES correlations by a small amount. This doesn't mean the bounds 
  are uninformative — it means that given these BES marginals, the observed
  correlations are far from their theoretical limits, so the rescaling          
  adjustment is small. The practical case for rescaling needs to rest on
  comparability across pairs, not on magnitude of adjustment — because the
  adjustments are pair-specific and vary in sign (Figure 5b: asymmetry cuts both
   ways).

  ---
  Finding 5: The BES cloud has a distinctive "spur" structure (Figure 1)
                                                                                
  Most pairs cluster near (r_min ≈ −0.95, r_max ≈ 0.95), but there is a
  horizontal spur extending to r_min ≈ −0.3 while r_max stays near 0.9. This    
  means some variables have a high maximum but a much weaker lower bound — their
   anti-comonotonic pairing simply cannot achieve a very negative correlation.  
  This is the structure worth examining more closely: what types of variables
  produce the spur?
